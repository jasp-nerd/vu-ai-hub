About this course

This course presents an introduction to the various aspects of process modelling, using examples of relevant aspects of problems within the domains fo psychology, bio-medical science and social sciences. The course considers methodological aspects of modelling, such as: the collection and specification of relevant knowledge, the definition of relevant ontologies, the definition of simulation experiments and the validation of the results obtained from the modelling process. The course considers fundamental aspects of dynamical modelling methods, including: defining causal relationships and using causal graphs, defining/using executable specifications, differentiating and integrating quantitative and qualitative models.
 
In addition, the student learns how the various aspects of model design, implementation and evaluation can be used to build integrated systems following the agent-based modelling approach. The emphasis of the second half of the course is the application of domain models that can be used to address problems in a more realistic manner. By combining quantitative and qualitative model components, an integrative approach to problem description and analysis is supported.
The material in the course draws inspiration from a wide range of domains. These include: psychology (where emotions or moods are modelled), bio-medicine (where physiological models of the body are used to measure intoxication), or sociology and/or economics (where information transfer through social networks is studied).

Assignments

During the course, students will do weekly assignments based on the material discussed during the lectures (5 in total). The course is concluded with a larger assignment, in which students have to construct a model themselves. The student will be challenged to relate the models to relevant external input information (including, where appropriate, sensor-based inputs). In addition, a programming tutorial is a required element of the course.

Grading

The final grade will be determined by 1) the average results weekly assignments ( avg(A) ) except the programming tutorial (A3), which needs to be sufficient, 2) the results of the final assignment ( F ) and 3) the final exam (E).

The final grade = 0.4 * avg(A) + 0.2 * F + 0.4 * E
if all partial grades are > 5.5.

When failing to meet the deadline, 1 point will be subtracted of the grade of that assignment per missed day.
