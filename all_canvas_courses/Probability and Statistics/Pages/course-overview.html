Learning goals

General learning goals: thorough understanding of elementary probability theory and statistical inference.

After this course the student should be able to:

  • understand the setup of a probability space, events, probability and conditional probability;
  • use the basic rules of probability theory (sum rule, product rule, law of total probability, Bayes' rule);
  • understand what a random variable is, what its distribution is, both for discrete and continuous random variables (this includes the concepts probability mass function, probability density function and cumulative distribution function);
  • compute the expectation and variance of a random variable;
  • compute the mean and variance of a sum of independent random variables;
  • derive discrete probability distributions for probabilistic experiments associated with the Bernoulli, Binomial, Pascal and Poisson distribution;
  • compute probabilities for continuous random variables, specifically the uniform, exponential and normal distribution;
  • perform calculations based on the law of large numbers and the central limit theorem;
  • reproduce the definition of an estimator, do elementary computations on point estimators;
  • understand what a confidence interval is and derive it in simple settings using a pivot;
  • understand the setup of classical hypothesis testing (critical region, p-value, type of errors) and apply this to simple hypothesis testing problems;
  • derive the likelihood ratio test in simple settings;

Content

Textbook: Hossein Pishro-Nik, Introduction to Probability, Statistics and Random Processes (www.probabilitycourse.com).

Chapter 1: Basic concepts.

Chapter 3: Discrete random variables and special distributions (Bernoulli, Binomial, Pascal, Poisson).

Chapter 4: Continuous random variables; Section 4.1 and from Section 4.2: uniform, exponential, and normal distribution.

Chapter 7: Section 7.1 (the law of large numbers and the central limit theorem).

Chapter 8: Section 8.1-8.4 (point estimation, interval estimation, hypothesis testing).
