Syllabus — Multi-Agent Systems

Welcome to MAS! In this course, we will discuss knowledge representation and reasoning techniques, as well as multi-agent technology. Multi-agent systems are collections of autonomous agents that interact to solve problems more efficiently than a single agent could. They’re useful for modelling complex and dynamic real-world problems. From this course, you will gain practical and theoretical knowledge on how to build such a system.

 

Course Objectives - What Will Your Takeaway Be?

  1. Agents and Multi-Agent Systems

    Agent, multi-agent system, cognitive state, beliefs, goals, actions, communication, coordination. You will be able to define the elementary concepts, and to apply their relevant aspects in the design of programs. (Learning outcomes: 'Knowledge and understanding', 'Applying knowledge and understanding')

  2. Agent-Environment Interaction

    Action, percept. You will be capable of analysing the environment in which the agent operates, and to identify the actions and relevant percepts that are available. You will also be able to use the classification, actions and percepts as the basis for the design of an agent. (Learning outcomes: 'Knowledge and understanding', 'Applying knowledge and understanding')

  3. Agent and Multi-Agent Program

    Action rules, modules, MAS file, launch rules. You will learn to describe the relevant programming constructs. In addition, you will be able to apply the constructs to write a multi-agent and agent programs. (Learning outcome: 'Applying knowledge and understanding')

  4. Basic Concepts Prolog

    Facts, rules, clause, queries, rule-based reasoning. You will learn to define the basic concepts in Prolog and describe the relationship between these concepts. Based on these concepts you will also have the ability to solve simple problems in Prolog. (Learning outcomes: 'Applying knowledge and understanding, 'Making judgments')

  5. Prolog Programming

    Negation as failure, cut, recursion, lists. You will learn to apply constructs and these techniques to write a Prolog program. You will also learn to solve problems by using a combination of negation as failure, recursion and the use of lists. In particular, you will be able to implement some search algorithms in Prolog. (Learning outcome: 'Applying knowledge and understanding')

  6. Reasoning in Logic Programming

    Unification, backtracking, depth-first search, linear search, backward chaining. You will know how to explain the computational model of logic and reasoning in Prolog and to use these concepts. You will learn to complete simple tasks with unification of terms. You will also be able to construct a derivation of a unification (resolution). (Learning outcomes: 'Applying knowledge and understanding', 'Communication')

  7. Develop a MAS

    You will have the ability to build a relatively simple multi-agent system. Concepts relating to systems of rational agents are introduced to make complex decisions. (Learning outcomes: 'Applying knowledge and understanding', 'Lifelong learning skills')

Assessment

The course is assessed in two parts, through an exam and a practical part (assignments). In order to pass the course, you must: 1. pass each of these two parts, and 2. obtain a final grade of at least 55/100. Your final grade will be a weighted average of your exam grade (75%) and your grade for the practical part (25%). 

  • Practical part: You will get 3 practical assignments that must be completed in pairs. The assignments are graded with a mark (0-100). The overall grade for the practical part is the average of the marks for the assignments. In order to pass the practical part, your average must be at least 50/100.
  • Exam: this exam will be at the end of the course. It will primarily contain multiple choice and programming questions. In order to pass it, you must also score at least 50/100 on the exam. 

Other requirements:

  • Attendance: Attending the lab/practical sessions is mandatory. You will however be allowed 2 absences in case of unforeseeable circumstances. It is your responsibility to use them wisely, and not end up in a situation where you don't have any left when you really need one, resulting in failing the course. When using an absence you do not have to let us know, but please do inform your lab partner. If you are more than 15 minutes late, you will be marked late (50% attendance). You can only leave early when there is no more work to be done (e.g. you submitted all the published assignments). If you leave early in all other situations, you will be marked with 50% attendance.
    To recap:     in order to pass the course, you must have at least 5/7 attendance for practical sessions. 
  • Assignment 0: In addition to the 3 assignments, you must complete assignment 0 at the beginning of the course, which steps you through the installation of Prolog and MARBEL. This is to ensure everyone has the proper setup for the rest of the course. You get a pass or a 'not complete' for this assignment, but it will not be included in your final grade. If you don't complete this assignment you immediately fail the course.

Second chances:

  • There will be a resit for the exam
  • There is no second chance for the practical assignments

Late submissions: If you submit your assignment up to 24h after the deadline, you will have a 1 point deduction from your grade (out of 10). If you submit your assignment more that one day after the deadline, you will receive a 0 on that assignment. 

 

Grade Transfers

If you have taken the MAS course before, you are allowed to transfer some of your previous grades, as to not have to redo all assignments/exam. If you wish to do so, you must fill in this Google form before October 29th. Submissions after this deadline will not be taken into account. Note that you have to sign in with your VU credentials in order to gain access to the form.

Please read the following rules before filling out this form:

  • Each assignment/exam you want to transfer has to be >=5.0.
  • Once the above form is submitted, the decision is final.
    • If you submit an assignment you requested a grade transfer for, it will not be graded.
    • If you take the exam which you requested a grade transfer for, it will not be graded.
  • If you only transfer the exam grade, you have to do the assignments in the normal way (i.e. join a group, attend the mandatory practical sessions).
  • If you transfer all the assignments, you are excused from the mandatory practicals and do not join a group. You only do the exam.
  • If you partially transfer assignment grades, you will have to submit the missing assignments. You are exempt from the mandatory attendance, but are highly encouraged to join the practicals that focus on the assignments you still have to do. Depending on the number of students, we might ask you to do these assignments alone. If we cannot pair you with someone, you will be informed of this shortly after the start of the course. If you are unwilling to work by yourself, we suggest that you do not transfer any assignment grades.
  • It is your responsibility to make sure your transfer request is valid (for instance, if you indicate the wrong year and we cannot find your grade, it will not be transferred and you will have to re-do the assignment.)
