Implement your Hi-Fi prototype
During this final week, you are expected to create the Hi-Fi prototype for your human-robot/agent interaction based on the outcome of last week's pilot experiment. Here, you must incorporate the improvements you identified in Assignment 4 - question 4a, but you are free to make additional changes as you see fit. 

Note that the most suitable prototype for this assignment is the one that best supports you in conducting your user experiment. This means that you should focus on implementing interaction details that are relevant to your variables and improving the internal validity of your experiment. Adding other cool interaction elements unrelated to your variables may, at best, improve the external validity, but is likely to decrease the internal validity of your experiment.

 

Conduct a User Study with the Hi-Fi Prototype

In the context of your group project, you must design and conduct a scientific experiment using your high-fidelity prototype. This user study should answer a scientific RQ and provide valuable information for future researchers who attempt a human-robot/agent interaction design within the same theme you chose for your project. The study can target any aspect of the user experience, such as design and usability issues, user perception of the agent before and after the interaction, user engagement, user learning, conformity, trust scores, response times, task performance, or perception of different user groups when they interact with the agent. The Hi-Fi prototype used in this study must be significantly different (i.e., improved) from the Lo-Fi prototype. 

Similar to the pilot study you conducted last week, here before you start data collection you must first determine a) the aspects of the user experience that you plan to study, b) the dependent and independent variables, and consequently the experimental conditions, c) the methods and study protocol of your experiment, and d) the data analysis and reporting format. 

Some of these items (such as variables and experimental conditions) you have already defined in previous assignments. Therefore, in this assignment, you will focus on other implementation aspects of the user study, e.g., the needed materials, the finalized protocol for the experiment, and ethical considerations.

Conduct your user study with a minimum of 8 subjects. Make sure that you collect an ethical consent form from your users prior to participation. This form can be based on previous examples from literature (Also see materials shared under Modules > Research Project)

 

Write the report:

Using the provided template here, write a final report of your group project (max 5 pages excluding References). The page limit must be adhered to! 

The report should include a description of the use case and problem statement, a review of relevant literature, your RQ, hypothesis and proposed solution(s), the final implementation, the experiment procedure of the user study, the evaluation metrics, the results (including the statistical analysis and visualization) and finally a discussion of the limitations and future improvements of your design. When writing this report, as can be seen in the template, you must follow the structure of scientific publications, outlined in Chapter 8.2 of the book: 

  • 8.2.1 Title
  • 8.2.2 Abstract
  • 8.2.3 Keywords
  • 8.2.4 Introduction
  • 8.2.5 Methods
  • 8.2.6 Results and Discussion
  • 8.2.7 Conclusion
  • 8.2.8 References
  • (You must also include the Statement of Contribution in your report. Including an Acknowledgement section is optional.)

 

Some hints to not forget!

Introduction:

  • Describe the purpose of your user study. What effect are you trying to achieve with your interaction design? What problem are you going to solve? What are your RQ(s) and hypotheses? What are the past studies that support your RQ and hypothesis?

Methods:

  • Present the details of your study methodology and protocol. State your study design (between-subject, within-subject) and all conditions (independent variables) that you are going to implement (in what order?). Also, think about uncontrolled variables (factors that might influence your results, but cannot be controlled by you) and discuss how you'll deal with them in your study. 
  • Describe the apparatus you are going to use during your study. List ALL material you need for running your experiment, e.g.,  Consent form, Questionnaires (in digital form or pens & paper?), Audio recorder, etc. 
  • Data collection & Measurements. Describe your target sample and sampling method. How many participants will you need? What kind of data will you collect during your study? Describe the variables you will be measuring; reflect how each individual measurement will help you answer your research question or validate your hypothesis. How will you collect and store the data?

Results:

  • Compile appropriate data visualizations (tables, graphs, ...) in your project report. 
  • Perform the relevant statistical analyses on the data collected in this user study. 

Discussion:

  • Try to link your findings with previously published studies. How is your study different? What results did you gain that others did not? do you see similarities or differences between your results and results of past studies?
