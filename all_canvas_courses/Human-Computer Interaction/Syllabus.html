Aims

In many application domains, AI systems must collect input from humans or collaborate with them in order to enhance their performance and decision-making processes. This course will introduce students in the AI program to the existing theories and methods for the design and evaluation of interactions between humans and [intelligent, socially interactive, or immersive] computing systems. The aim is to give students an understanding of the concepts of usability, user experience (UX), and user-centered design, and to equip them with empirical research skills for prototyping and iterative testing of such interactive computing systems. 

The course is divided into two parts, each achieving different learning goals;

In the theoretical part of the course (lectures), students will learn about the core topics in HCI such as: the lifecycle of interaction design, human factors, interaction elements, and common research methods in HCI studies.

In the practical part of the course, students will acquire hands-on research experience where they collaborate in groups to design, conduct, and report on an HCI experiment with human participants.

It will consist of several close-ended questions (e.g., multiple choice, fill in the blanks, true/false, etc.) testing the knowledge and skills taught in the course. You are expected to recognize the correct or best answer among multiple other answers that are incorrect and/or less complete. 

More details about the exam will be published in the future under Modules.

 

Group Project

For the practical part of this course (in May), you must collaborate in teams to 1) define a relevant HCI problem within the specified themes, 2) design an interaction that would provide a solution to the problem, 3) evaluate your interaction design in a controlled user study (with a focus on user needs/profiles or interaction elements), and 4) write a report based on your experiment outcomes.

To maintain structure and streamline the implementation phase among all groups, the practicals will focus on designing a "social interaction" between humans and embodied agents using the Furhat Robot (Virtual Furhat tool). There are three themes that groups can select to focus their problem statement on; Education and learning, Healthcare and therapy, and Customer service. See more details and examples of the project themes here.

ALL you need to know about developing a human-agent interaction with the Virtual Furhat tool and conducting user studies will be shared under Modules > Research projects. This module will be expanded throughout the course as we publish new materials.

The groups are formed by the students themselves. Every team should have exactly 4 members. 

You can already start forming your teams and discussing potential topics for your project in the first part of the course. The official registration of teams is in Week 5 after the exam.

There are specific HCI Teams at the bottom of the list made for retake groups. If you are retaking the course, please assign yourself to one of those teams named "Resit Team #". Also, if you are a retake student, please form a group with other retake students and do not mix with first-year students.

 

Assignments & Grading

There are a total of 4 assignments given throughout the course; 2 individual assignments and 2 group assignments.

The 2 individual assignments are given in the first part of the course and include questions related to the lectures' content. These assignments are very useful for your exam preparation.

The 2 group assignments are given in the second part of the course and include questions related to your progress on the group project. These assignments are useful in formulating your research question for the group project and advancing your user study for the final project report.

ALL 4 assignments are mandatory and serve as the prerequisite for your exam/project report to be graded, however, they will only receive Complete/Incomplete grades according to the rubrics. If one or more of the 4 assignments are missing or if you received an Incomplete grade on at least one of them, your exam or final report will not be graded (see Late and Incomplete Assignment Policy below).

* Please note that assignments in this course serve as formative assessments to ensure that you follow the lecture content and make progress on your group project. The goal is for you to attempt the questions and receive feedback from lecturers and TAs at every stage. That is why we will only grade the assignments as complete vs. incomplete, with rubrics that focus on evaluating your effort rather than the accuracy of your answers. 

Assignment deadlines & Penalties

Deadlines: Assignments 1 and 2 will be published at the end of Week 1 and 2 and will have a submission deadline on the following Fridays. Therefore, you have 1 week to work on each assignment. Assignments 3 and 4 will be published at the beginning of Weeks 6 and 7 (to give you direction for the practical sessions) and will have a deadline on the following Mondays. Again you have 1 week of time to work on each of these assignments and prepare the report together with your team.

Late assignment policy: We have strict deadlines for assignment submissions. If you do not meet the deadline, we will apply a penalty of 5% (-0.5 points) to your final grade if the assignment is still handed in within 24 hrs of the deadline and if it is graded Complete. After that, the assignment will not be graded, meaning you will lose eligibility for grading the written exam or the final project report.

Incomplete assignment policy: If you submit the assignment report on time, but your answers to some questions are missing or insufficient, your assignment will be graded "Incomplete". You don't fail the course immediately due to one Incomplete grade. In this case, you will be given a new deadline (max 5 working days) to resubmit a revised version of the report that is complete. However, in this case, we will apply a penalty of 5% (-0.5 points) to your final grade.

We are unfortunately not able to consider requests for extensions. Please do not send emails related to deadline extensions, as they won't be considered. Should you have confidential, health-related, or other long-term issues, the academic advisor is better suited to discuss your personal circumstances ( academicadvice.cs.beta@vu.nl).

Plagiarism and Free-riding

We take both plagiarism and free-riding very seriously in this course!!!

Free-riding refers to the act of not contributing equally to a group assignment, for instance when one or more members of a team do not participate equally (or at all) in the tasks but expect to receive an equal grade as other members. This is a common problem among groups that do not communicate effectively throughout the project and can lead to interpersonal conflicts and delays in the group's progress.

Plagiarism refers to the act of using someone else’s work (e.g., text, illustration, videos, etc.) without references and presenting it as one's own idea/work. This is often seen in research reports where text or figures from other papers are included without proper paraphrasing and/or citation.

  • To avoid free-riding, all groups are required to include a statement of contribution in their weekly assignment reports and their final project report to clarify who did what (see examples here). We advise teams to try and address any problems associated with free-riding within the team as early as possible into the project. If you are unable to resolve the issue, please communicate it to your TAs so that instructors can make timely decisions to assist the group.
  • To avoid plagiarism, you must write your original text even when you are communicating previously reported results. You can do this by summarizing or paraphrasing existing text and you should cite your sources (see examples here). It is okay to use ChatGPT or other generative AI tools for paraphrasing text but you should always include a statement of technology use in your report if you do so (see examples here and here). Please note that all cases of suspected plagiarism in the assignments and final project report will be reported to the Board of Examiners. Please consult the Teaching and Examination Regulations at the VU for more information.
