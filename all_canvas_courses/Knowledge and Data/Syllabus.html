Syllabus — Knowledge and Data

Welcome to the Knowledge and Data 2025 course !

(note that parts of this Canvas page are still under construction)

In this course you will study formalisms that are useful and necessary to represent knowledge and data, in particular when this knowledge and data is to be reused, e.g. published on the web. We introduce the Semantic Web stack, a set of technologies and representation formats (RDF, RDFS, OWL) for expressing semantics and linked data in a web-accessible format, use the SPARQL query language to query over this data, and build a Data science pipeline that uses integrated data and knowledge.

Course Setup

A more extended motivation of the setup of the course is found here, but here are the essentials:

  • All the relevant content will be provided both as live lectures (Mondays and Wednesdays) and through online video lectures and material. The Monday lecture will introduce the content of each module and the Wednesday lecture will be used both for deepening content and Q&A
  • The course consists of two main components:
    • 5 Modules of practice and theory (2/3 of the grade): in the first 5 weeks you get to understand the problem and appropriate solutions through lectures, working groups and practical assignments. The practical assignments count for 1/3 of the grade.
      • To progress to a next module, you will need to pass a short (ungraded) digital quiz in Canvas. 
      • There will also be a digital multiple choice exam testing your knowledge of these five modules, counting towards the other 1/3. (See Exam below).
    • Final project (1/3 of the grade) : In the last 3 weeks of the course there will be a practical assignment. In this project you will work in groups of 4 people, and apply all the skills you have learned in the weeks before.
  • What do you have to do:
    • Attend the on-site lectures. These will not be streamed but they are recorded and recordings+slides will be made available on the Lecture pages in the Week 1-5 modules.
    • Self-study using the video and written material on Canvas. The videos are also found on Youtube.
    • Attend your working group (on Fridays) to help you get familiar with the quiz and exam material. 
    • Attend your practical session (on Tuesdays) to get help from the TAs on the practical assignments
      • Alternatively/additionally you can book an online meeting with a TA: https://tinyurl.com/kandd25onlineTA 
    • Make the weekly Canvas quiz to pass the module and get access to the next one
    • Hand in weekly practical assignments by the deadline specified.
      • For 2 out of the 5 practical assignments you have to book an appointment for a coding interview with a TA in the week after receiving your grade. Check the grading page for more information. 

      • About deadline extensions: We can not extend the assignment deadlines for individual cases. our hard rule is: anything handed in after the deadline but within 24hrs will be graded but with -1pt. Anything after that will receive a fail (1). If you have an exceptional reason to ask for such an extension (family emergency, serious medical accidents etc), you can contact your study advisor with the specific situation.  All other requests will not be considered.

Blended learning setup 2025

This course implements VU's blended learning strategy and has onsite and online components:

  1. lectures will be given on-site. The recordings and slides will be made available after the lecture. 
  2. working groups will be given on-site, they will not be streamed or recorded. We will make the assignments and solutions available after each lecture
  3. scheduled practical sessions will be given on-site, they will not be streamed or recorded. We additionally have (limited) online support.

Note that attendance to the lectures, working groups, or practical sessions is not mandatory, yet strongly recommended.

Rules on collaboration and using (AI) tools

For the individual assignments, we allow and even encourage that students discuss between them at high level the assignments and strategies of solving them as well as provide help on specific issues. However, the assignments are individual and duplicating / copying entire solutions is considered fraud and will be reported to the examination board. For the individual practical assignments, use of (generative) AI tools is not allowed. 

For the group assignment, please be aware that acquiring active writing, designing, implementation and reflection skills is an important part of your academic training. The use of AI as a supportive tool towards the project document text (for brainstorming, improving written text etc) is allowed for the group assignment but should only be in support of, not as a replacement for these skills. When you use AI for the group assignment, acknowledge your use and report how it affects your assignment. Failure to do so is also considered violation of the course rules.  You will always be held accountable for the correctness, completeness, and coherence of all your texts.

Use of such tools is not allowed for the other parts of the group assignment (including knowledge engineering).   

In case there is doubt about the originality/individuality of any contribution, we can ask for an oral check for that assignment. (see also the grading page).
