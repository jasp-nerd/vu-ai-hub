 Instructions for students for Assignment 2 (deadline 25 April)

Important:
Check who your seminar instructor is. Incorrect submissions will not be graded.
Stick to the word limit and the requirements (all submissions without a reflection, submissions with a three-sentence introduction, submissions with more than one body paragraph, and so on, will receive a failed grade).
Include your name in the document name and in the document itself.
You will be graded on linguistic range and accuracy, academic formality, source use and text structure, so basically what we covered so far. 

Assignment:

  1. Develop your 3-sentence ‘elevator pitch’ into a full introduction (1 paragraph of about 200 words that ends with your thesis statement), and write up the first body paragraph of your essay. The aim here is to see how you develop structure, how you follow quoting and referring conventions (including the use of a third-person perspective), and how you apply academic formality and linguistic accuracy in fully written up paragraphs.
  2. Use what you learned about structure in week 2, as well as the feedback you received on Assignment 1.
  3. The text should be written in a third-person perspective (Smith describes, concludes, points out etc.) and your layout should mirror what we discussed in class.
  4. Include a bibliography, according to IEEE or Harvard style, with the two articles that you want to use for the final assignment:

- for instructions on Harvard, see: https://dkit.ie.libguides.com/harvard/citing-referencing  

- for instructions on IEEE, see https://pitt.libguides.com/citationhelp/ieee

  1. Apart from the introduction and first body paragraph, write a few sentences each on (i) how you plan to develop the paper further, and (ii) on what you want to learn from this assignment. You can include one or two specific aspects you want feedback on in particular from your instructor.
  2. The text should contain 400 words with a 10% margin, references and outline (how you plan to develop the paper and what you want to learn from it) excluded.
  3. Do not use AI writing tools to generate the content. In the course we consider their uses for editing.

 

Some general tips:

1) Study well-edited published examples (e.g., https://www.nature.com)

2) Use a collocations dictionary, such as OZDIC.com

3) Use the Chicago Manual of Style (but only for questions concerning grammar and punctuation) and check every comma

4) Use the Academic Phrasebank: https://www.phrasebank.manchester.ac.uk/
